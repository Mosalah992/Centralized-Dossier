## Background

*Setting premise, supplied directly rather than extracted from the images. Kept
separate from the transcriptions so the provenance of each stays clear.*

Twenty-five years have passed since the Stormcloaks first rose. They took
Solitude once, but held it briefly: the assassinations of **Vittoria Vici** and
**Emperor Titus Mede II** ended the Elder Council's restraint, and a fully
equipped veteran Imperial force was committed to Skyrim. Against a true Legion,
Ulfric's armies were crushed. The Empire dismantled the rebellion, occupied the
holds, and imposed a martial reconstruction that has run for decades.

Then Imperial priorities moved on. With the province secure and a standing army
so far from Cyrodiil proving expensive, the Council withdrew its elites and left
a cheaper occupation in their place — **a patchwork auxiliary legion of local
recruits, mercenaries, and a handful of Imperial officers**.

The Stormcloak spirit did not die with the rebellion. What survived reorganised
into an insurgency of small cells, hidden camps and guerrilla fighters scattered
through the mountains, and after years of quiet it has begun striking again with
coordinated precision. Raids, ambushes and sabotage grow bolder; the auxiliary
legion, undertrained and politically hamstrung, is struggling to hold what the
Empire believed was settled.

### How the papers bear this out

The reporting is consistent with that premise at every point where it touches:

- **The twenty-five years are counted openly.** The Riften Courier opens
  Thundercaller's bid with *"Exactly twenty-five years after civil war tore our
  lands apart, the specter of bloody conflict has returned to the gates of
  Solitude,"* and its special report contrasts the coming war with *"the conflict
  of 25 years prior."*
- **The insurgency is visible as "rebels."** Faric of Highrock's assault on
  Solitude with an army of 30 (Second Seed 4), described as ununified and
  crazed, reads as exactly the sort of cell-scale strike the premise describes —
  as does the vampire-and-bandit-plagued state of the roads.
- **The auxiliary legion's weakness shows.** On Midyear 4 the Legion assaults
  Windhelm's Palace of the Kings on three fronts — and **loses**, its
  legionnaires defeated and captured. A veteran Imperial force would not have
  been expected to break against a single hold.
- **Occupation politics are still live.** The Legion seizes the Bards College as
  barracks; the Empire dissolves Riften's court outright and orders its guard to
  stand down; a Green Veil company operates out of an abandoned Stormcloak camp.
- **And the rebellion stops hiding.** Thundercaller's bid is the pivot: rather
  than a hidden cell, a sitting Jarl declares for High King before Solitude's
  gates, backed by two holds' armies, **invoking the name of Talos** to roaring
  chants. The Courier notes it is "sure to stoke the fires of political unrest
  with Imperial sympathizers and The Dominion."

What the massacre supplies is the pretext. An insurgency testing a weak
occupation is a slow problem; a hold's military slaughtering an allied clan under
a peace banner, with an Imperial order behind it, is a cause. The papers record
the moment the second civil war stops being a security matter and becomes a
legitimate one.

## The central thread: the road to civil war

The four papers together tell one story, and it is not the werewolves.

1. **Provocation.** The Vashu'agra clan is barred from Whiterun and Solitude over
   crimes they insist were committed by impostors and by two exiles they had
   taken in and since disavowed. They repeatedly seek reconciliation.
2. **Escalation.** Bloodkin **Kit'ra Fernwind** is arrested. The clan mobilises;
   Housecarl **Kobra** brokers her release for banishment. The Riften Courier
   calls it *"our first steps towards peace."*
3. **The trap.** On Seed 23 the clan is invited to Solitude for formal peace
   talks. Leaders are admitted to the Blue Palace; their guard is barred outside.
   Whiterun guards — far outside their jurisdiction, uncontested by Solitude's —
   surround them, and on Imperial authority the order is given: *"Kill them all!"*
4. **The cost.** Chieftain Largbur dead, the leadership annihilated, survivors
   tortured. Guards reportedly began removing helmets and ordering the death of
   **any** Orsimir present regardless of affiliation. Jarl Florian dies of wounds
   taken in his own court.
5. **The rupture.** Windhelm and Riften, the clan's long-standing allies, are
   outraged. Within three days a combined eastern host stands at Solitude's gates
   and **Thorgil Thundercaller declares for High King**, invoking Talos.
6. **Open war.** By Midyear 4 the Empire has dissolved Riften's court, and the
   Legion has assaulted Windhelm's Palace of the Kings on three fronts — and
   lost.

The Riften Courier's framing: *"Unlike the conflict of 25 years prior, this would
be a brutal, geographic affair between the polar ends of the province."*

## Thalmor and Dominion coverage

Collected because it bears directly on this project's own records. The press
portrait is not flattering, and not uniform.

**As defenders.** At the Siege of Solitude (Seed 4) the *Tamriel Tribune*
credits the Thalmor with reinforcing the city gates and holding the frontline
against Faric of Highrock, and with interrogating him at Riften afterwards. This
is the only unambiguously favourable Thalmor coverage in the whole set.

**As suspects.** On Seed 15 the *Riften Courier* runs **"Wolves in Gold
Clothing?"** — a rumour, championed by a premier monster-hunting guild, that the
Thalmor are orchestrating the werewolf surge. The paper is openly sceptical,
noting the theory sits badly with Thalmor discipline, but reports that
anti-Dominion groups are using rural grief as a recruitment tool toward another
civil war. One quoted hunter: *"I KNOW THE THALMOR ARE WEREWOLVES! THEY WANT US
HUNTING BLIND IN THE FOREST WHILE THEY SEIZE SKYRIM."*

**As a protection racket.** On Seed 28 an Aldmeri "diplomat" appears in Riften
town square offering any "citizen of the Dominion" safekeeping of their septims
for a flat **10 gold a month**, with a **Thalmor escort** for purchases over 200
septims. The Courier reports citizens are "rightfully skeptical" and asks whether
this is stabilisation or "calculated overreach by the Aldmeri Dominion."

**As a peacekeeper.** The *Haafingar Gazette* #2 records the **Second Emissary of
the Thalmor and his entourage** attending the Khajiit Pilgrimage of the Moons in
the Reach "to keep the peace", alongside the Vigilants, the Dawnguard and
Markarth's Dar'Mozahr. The sealed temple opened to the Khajiit that night.

**As a byword.** Thundercaller's Talos-invoking bid for High King is noted as
"sure to stoke the fires of political unrest with Imperial sympathizers and The
Dominion." And the Gazette's editor, condemning Whiterun's conduct at the
massacre: *"It is utterly disgusting and something I'd see in The Dominion rather
than here."*

## Per-paper notes

### The Tamriel Tribune — Second Seed 3, 5

Editor-in-chief **Trisiphine**, Lead Reporter **Niris Felstar**. 5 septims.
Covered in full in the chronology above. Its two issues are the earliest material
in the set — and both were printed **before its entire masthead died** in the
Bards College purge on Seed 21 (see below).

Issue 2's lighter material: Grinwald Gooseberry's travelling circus, with Victor
the bard, **Skitters the World-Renowned Jester**, Yarric and Bedwin's staging of
*The Lusty Argonian Maid*, and Weaver-of-Roots' oration of *"Olaf and the Golden
Canary."* Victor's *"Ballad of the Flying Horker"* caused the audience to form a
mosh pit. Also the Bronze Water Cave levy — 50 septims per group and one third of
all finds, by order of the Court of Eastmarch, signed by Steward **Haryn
Selvanni**.

### The Riften Courier — Second Seed 14–28

Masthead: *"News from the Ratway to the Blue Palace."* Runs **Chort's Advice
Corner** in every issue — a recurring comic column of brutally literal survival
and cooking advice from an orc ("If bear wants to eat Chort, Chort hits it with
rock. Chort eats bear after.").

Notable beyond the chronology:

- **Blood and Honey** (Seed 16) — the history of Goldenglow Estate. Previous
  owner **Duvall Telvanni** held a honey monopoly through his merchants'
  alliance, dealt skooma, and courted Molag Bal cultists intending to build an
  elite vampire guard. An anonymous whistleblower gathered evidence; he was
  executed for attempted vampirism, Molag Bal worship, conspiracy to assassinate
  the Jarl, skooma dealing and extortion.
- **Monster Hunters: Protectors or Pretenders?** (Seed 20) — the Vigilants,
  Dawnguard and Silver Dawn accused of barging into towns, seizing properties and
  building camps without local consent. Falkreath's leadership defends them.
- **The Riften Soup** (Seed 20) — the city's canal has become an open sewer;
  citizens have drowned in it.
- **A Final Ballad** (Seed 24) — the fullest account of the Bards College
  collapse. Headmaster **Petey Steive** was not himself accused of Morag Tong
  membership; he was killed for having known of Trisiphine and Niris' allegiance
  and failing to report it. The newly established Dunmer Embassy was suggested as
  involved; the Embassy denies it and has promised to execute any Morag Tong
  found operating in Skyrim.
- **Special Report** (Seed 26) — an obituary for **Jephrey "the Survivor"
  Pringle**, Champion of Meridia, "seven-foot-tall of saxhleel and twelve-inches
  of horn." A second copy of the same sheet was posted, hand-annotated by Pringle
  himself: an arrow to the corpse labelled **"ME!"** and his name scrawled across
  the second photograph. He is writing a column again by Midyear 15.

### The Haafingar Gazette — Second Seed 23, 26

Editor in Chief **Naril Laretheus**, "Dedicated to **Niris Hlaalu**". Established
4E 216, 5 septims. *"Skyrim's Go-To Printing and Publishing Company."*

The Gazette is a Solitude paper and its account of the massacre is the angriest
of the four. Its dead-list for the Bards College is the fullest: **Niris Hlaalu,
Trisiphine, Wayde Chateau-Legends, Gugnir Blue-Blood, Picks-Flowers, and Petey
Stevie**. It reads the court's public mourning as "one final insult to college by
the court of Solitude."

Its editor blames Whiterun without reservation — *"the one instigating the orcs
outside The Blue wasn't a Solitude Guard, not an Imperial, but it was a Whiterun
Soldier"* — and closes by calling for a moot.

### The Morthal Courier — Midyear 2–15

The Riften Courier relaunched. Masthead: *"Your Guide Through The Mist."*
Distributed at the Morthal General Store and, in Imperial territory, with a pint
of mead at the Winking Skeever in Solitude.

- **Winter Finally Wakes** (Midyear 2) — Winterhold's first Winterwake festival
  after twenty-five years as "a ghost of its former self": a bard-off, pit
  fights, a clam-eating competition, a tax-free market, and the College's
  spectral guardians descending to walk among the crowd before withdrawing.
- **The Men of the Reach** (Midyear 7) — the Reachmen, deer-headed and
  draped in furs and skulls, are **not** the Forsworn. Shaman **Tar-Xil**:
  *"Forswoin but sect of radical Reachmen lead by old king Madanach. Not all
  Reachmen tribes Forsworn. We different, we lead by different lineage."* They
  hunt bandits who prey on travellers. *"Don't be bandit."*
- **The Green Veil** — self-proclaimed protectors, merchants and mercenaries
  based at an abandoned Stormcloak camp in the Reach. Green cloak included upon
  recruitment.
- **The Walk for the Wartorn and Weyleid** — reporter **Monty** walks every hold
  in one trip against the coming war: *"a world sharing in safe roads, united not
  as east or west, but as her children."*

## Discrepancies and gaps

1. **The clan's name is spelled five different ways across four papers.** This
   matters for [Honors.tsx](../web/src/views/Honors.tsx), which uses
   **"Voshu-Agra"**:

   | Source | Spelling |
   |---|---|
   | Tamriel Tribune #2 | Vasha Agra |
   | Riften Courier, Seed 19 | Vashu Agra |
   | Riften Courier, Seed 24 | Vashu-Agra |
   | Riften Courier, Special Story 1 | Vashu'agra |
   | **Haafingar Gazette #3** | **Voshu Agra** |
   | **This project** | **Voshu-Agra** |

   **The archive's spelling is attested** — the Haafingar Gazette matches it
   bar the hyphen. My earlier note in the Tribune file, that the archive spelling
   was unsupported, was wrong; only the Tribune's "Vasha" is a clear outlier.

2. **Niris' surname changes between papers.** The Tamriel Tribune's lead reporter
   is **Niris Felstar**; the Haafingar Gazette's dead-list and dedication say
   **Niris Hlaalu**. Same person by role, date of death and college affiliation.

3. **Jephrey Pringle's death does not hold.** His obituary runs Second Seed 26;
   he annotates a copy of it himself, and writes a column in the Morthal Courier
   on Midyear 15.

4. **The Tamriel Tribune's 1st Issue is missing.** Issue 2 refers back to "last
   issue's headline article" about Grommash.

5. **The Morthal orc raid claims Largashbur allegiance** (Midyear 3), days after
   Largashbur stood as the Vashu'agra's ally at the fatal summit. The Courier
   flags this as unexplained.

6. **Whose orders?** The Riften Courier says Imperial authorities gave the order
   at the Blue Palace; the Haafingar Gazette insists the instigator "wasn't a
   Solitude Guard, not an Imperial, but a Whiterun Soldier." Both agree Whiterun's
   military entered outside its jurisdiction.

7. **Legibility.** All Riften/Morthal Courier issues (800px wide), both Gazette
   issues (1650×2550) and Tribune #2 (2500×4886) are clean. **Tribune #3 is only
   491×960**; its headline and lead article are certain, its small columns were
   read from a 4× upscale.
